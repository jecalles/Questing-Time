Welcome to Questing Time! This page is your quick-start guide. It is not the full GM notes — just what you need to build a character and play. This page covers only what an ordinary person would know. If a rumor appears here, your character has heard it. If something doesn't appear here, your character hasn't.

Use this to decide where you're from, who you know, why you read the questing board, and how to build your character.

# The World, Broadly

**New Squishnesta** — "the City." A techno-magical metropolis and the center of most people's world. It runs on farm produce and on magical services sold abroad, and it smells faintly of bubblegum. Nobody knows what happened to Old Squishnesta. Some say it never existed. See The City, below, for the landmarks every local knows.

**Squishnestashire** — "the Shire." The farm country surrounding the City, stretching all the way to the coast. Sparse, agrarian, and proud of it. The floating traincars that feed the City load up here. Shire folk are hearty, plainspoken, and suspicious of anything that interrupts the harvest.

**Sharp Forest** — a distant forest and bogland, named for the density and bite of its vegetation. Only its perimeter is settled, and sparsely. The Druids keep it, and they don't encourage visitors. Travelers' tales say the forest itself is alive, in a slow and creeping way — and that deep inside, where only the Wild ones live, there are humming stones and clockwork things older than anyone can name. Nobody who repeats these tales has seen any of it firsthand.

**The lands beyond** — the world is larger than the City and the Shire, but common folk know little of it. Folk say the dead go somewhere — past the River Styx, the stories claim, though no map shows the way. Now and then a stranger turns up in the City — an Orc, a Drau, one of the walking dead, even an infernal — living proof that other lands exist. Few can say where they hail from, and the strangers rarely volunteer it. Everyone knows there's a war on somewhere, and nobody can tell you against whom.

*Playing an Orc, Drau, Undead, Devil, or Demon? Your character knows their own homeland — more than this page tells. Work out those details with your GM.*

# Races

Each character holds one die of each size: D20, D12, D10, D8, D6, D4. You assign one die to each of your six stats (Prowess, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma, Instinct). Your race sets the starting pattern for that assignment.

**The Munchkin** — Humanoid small people, and a human subspecies with a trace of Elf blood too thin to give them magic. D20 Charisma, D12 Instinct, D10 Constitution, D8 Wisdom, D6 Intelligence, D4 Prowess. **Sweet Blood** — sugar and sweets hit you far harder than they hit anyone else; a dose that's nothing to a human can sedate, stimulate, or intoxicate you outright. Most psychedelics run the other way: something in your blood resists them, for reasons nobody's traced.

**High Elves** — Comical parodies of themselves. Long lives, grace and airs, a stagnant and decadent culture. D20 Wisdom, D12 Prowess, D10 Instinct, D8 Intelligence, D6 Constitution, D4 Charisma. **Ancient Instinct** — you sense the true alignment of anyone near you, no roll needed, and nobody can fake theirs past you.

**Humans** — Typically farmers and countryfolk. Salt of the earth, hearty, no-nonsense. D20 Constitution, D12 Charisma, D10 Prowess, D8 Instinct, D6 Intelligence, D4 Wisdom.

**Fey (Seelie)** — Trickster spirits and faeries. D20 Prowess, D12 Charisma, D10 Instinct, D8 Wisdom, D6 Intelligence, D4 Constitution. **Faerie's Grace** — react on Prowess or Instinct, whichever suits the moment. **Trickster** — your lies come easy and land well. You also carry **Ancient Instinct**, same as the elves above.

**The Undead** — Ghouls, zombies, ghosts. D20 Wisdom, D12 Instinct, D10 Prowess, D8 Charisma, D6 Intelligence, D4 Constitution. Playing one? Ask your GM what your racial ability does when you build your sheet.

**Orcs** — Big, hairy, and misunderstood. D20 Prowess, D12 Constitution, D10 Wisdom, D8 Charisma, D6 Instinct, D4 Intelligence.

**Drau** — A cave people, and opportunistic colonizers. Streetsmart and worldly next to the kin they left behind. D20 Instinct, D12 Wisdom, D10 Intelligence, D8 Charisma, D6 Constitution, D4 Prowess. **Night Vision** — you see perfectly in the dark, no light and no roll needed. Bright light costs you instead: roll Constitution before anything that depends on seeing, and a fail puts that check at disadvantage. Sunglasses cancel the penalty outright.

**Devils** — Infernal. Lawful evil to lawful neutral. D20 Charisma, D12 Intelligence, D10 Instinct, D8 Constitution, D6 Wisdom, D4 Prowess. **Ancient Instinct**, same as the elves above.

**Demons** — Infernal. Chaotic evil to chaotic neutral. The reverse of Devils. D20 Prowess, D12 Wisdom, D10 Constitution, D8 Instinct, D6 Intelligence, D4 Charisma. **Ancient Instinct**, same as the elves above.

# The City

New Squishnesta — everyone just calls it "the City" — is a techno-magical metropolis, landlocked, fed by floating traincars that bring in produce from Squishnestashire, the farm country around it. The City runs on farming and on magical services sold to other realms. It smells faintly of bubblegum.

A few places every local knows:

- **The Tavern** — the usual place to meet and pick up a job in person, once you've taken it from the board.
- **The Castle** — the seat of culture and power. Aristocratic, gaudy, insular.
- **The General Store** — a shop that sells a bit of everything.
- **The Questing Board** — the town-square board where jobs get posted. See "The Board," below.
- **The Red Line** — the City's poorest district, ringed by a road paved in red brick. A seedy ghetto, home mostly to Munchkins and to lesser and minor fey. Everyone knows there's illicit business behind its doors, and everyone knows better than to ask whose.
- **The Well** — a somewhat well-known landmark within the Red Line: a mysterious, magical well, and a congregating spot for feyfolk and their friends.

The City is landlocked; Squishnestashire, the farmland around it, reaches the coast.

Races you'll meet around the City: the Munchkin, High Elves, Humans, and Fey (Seelie) — see Races, above.

# Names Everyone Knows

These are the powers an ordinary person could name, and what they're known for. Your character might belong to one, owe one, or be avoiding one.

**The College of Mages** — the authority on Derived magic: what counts as canon, and who may practice it. The College admits mages of any race. Scholarly, exacting, and not to be plagiarized. It's also the closest thing the City has to a ruling power — everyone knows that, and everyone knows that crossing even a minor mage is considered your own fault. Its best-known public face is **Dean Alistair** of the Restoration school — old, kind, and endlessly readable.

**The Five Families** — everyone knows the Red Line answers to five crime families, even if nobody says so where a City official can hear. Two names come up more than the rest: **the Don**, who runs things the old way and expects to be treated with respect, and **Bill the Butcher**, sweet as anything, beloved by his own district, and not a man to cross.

**The Homesteaders** — the farm folk of the Shire and their kin in the City. Not so much an organization as a people. They want to be left alone, and they are slow to rouse — but loyal and fearsome when a cause finally aligns them.

**The Smuggler's Guild** — everyone has heard of the Guild, and most have bought from it once without knowing. It has a hand in every trade, legal or not, and it takes no side in any quarrel: money talks, and the Guild listens. Generally decent to common folk — except to any authority that refuses to bribe or be bribed.

**The Druids of the Forest** — the keepers of Sharp Forest and its creatures. Good-hearted in a wild, inconvenient way. If you must cross the Forest, ask them first.

**The High Coven** — a legend, maybe. Six ancient witches, each the head of her own coven, said to hold moot over all sorcerous practice and to enforce the old Taboos. No one has seen them convene, and many say they're a myth. Sorcerers, notably, do not laugh when the subject comes up.

# Magic, As Folk Understand It

Everyone knows magic is real. Most people sort its practitioners into three traditions:

**Innate magic** is born, not learned. The Illuminated races — Elves, Fey, celestials, infernals — carry it in their blood, and Sorcerers attune to it. It's taught the old way, master to apprentice, and improvement feels like barriers falling away.

**Derived magic** is studied. Wizards and Mages of any race build it from artifacts, alchemy, and the encantation of Power Words. It's more artifice than art, and the College keeps its canon.

**Gifted magic** is given. Warlocks, Clerics, and Druids receive theirs from higher powers, and keep it the way you keep any relationship: through devotion, community, and work.

One more piece of common belief: the Illuminated say their souls return after death, life after life. Whether that's cosmology or comfort is a question for the priests.

**The magic system is public, and it's yours.** This isn't secret GM lore you're not allowed to know — it's a shared, co-created part of the world, the same as any other setting fact. What's above is the folk version; the full mechanics — the Karmic Order, how the schools of magic relate, the pantheon of named powers — are written at [Magic System](../Lore/Magic%20System.md). Read it, ask your GM to walk through it with you, and treat it as something you help write, not something you're handed.

# The Board

Most towns keep one questing board; a major city may keep several. It's the standard way jobs get posted to adventurers.

- Anyone can pin a flyer to the board.
- You don't need an introduction to the poster. Take a flyer, and the job is yours.
- The Tavern is the usual spot to meet and formally accept a posted job in person, once you've picked it off the board.

# How Dice Work

Each character has six stats: Prowess, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma, Instinct. Each stat holds one of your six dice (D20 down to D4). The bigger the die, the better you are at that stat.

**Rolling.** To make a check, roll the die assigned to the relevant stat against a Difficulty (DC) the GM sets, or against another character's roll (an opposed check — for example, your Prowess to sneak against their Instinct to notice).

**Exploding dice.** If you roll the highest number on your die, it "explodes": roll again and add the new result to your total. Keep exploding as long as you keep hitting the max. Stop rolling the moment you've already succeeded.

**Advantage and disadvantage.** With advantage, roll your whole pool twice (each explodes fully) and take the higher total. With disadvantage, do the same and take the lower total.

**Planned vs. snap.** A Planned Action happens without time pressure, under good conditions — if the DC is half your die size or less, you succeed automatically, no roll needed. A Snap Decision is under pressure; you roll straight, and nobody can spend Determination Tokens to help you.

**Determination Tokens.** Every time you fail a check, you gain a token. Spend a token to add +1 to any roll, one token per point. Outside combat, other party members can spend tokens to help your Planned Action rolls too.

**Degrees of success.** Beating a DC by 10 or more is treated like a critical — narrate it as unbelievable. Beating it by 5 or more should look effortless. Missing by 5 or more has minor consequences; missing by 10 or more has serious ones.

Ask your GM for the full rules reference if you want more detail — this page covers just what you need to play.

# Building Your Character

A quick checklist:

1. **Pick a race** — stats and starting dice are in Races, above.
2. **Pick a home** — the City, the Red Line, the Shire, the Forest's edge, or somewhere stranger (talk to your GM).
3. **Pick a connection** — a faction above that your character belongs to, works for, resents, or owes.
4. **Pick a reason** — why does your character read the questing board? Coin, glory, escape, or something they're looking for.
