# Session Seven — the empty library, and the sand test

*Played 2026-10-15. Previously: Session Six, the bang and the library.*

The party woke at dawn in the old schoolhouse library to find it had lost
two people in the night. [Ember](../Player%20Sheets/Ember%20Spanner.md) had absconded, and with her most of
[Ada](../NPC%20Sheets/Ada%20Crane.md)'s papers. [Bob](../Player%20Sheets/Baeon%20"Bob"%20Oberoni.md)'s robes and gear sat folded in a corner, and there was
no body. [Pudge](../Player%20Sheets/Pudge.md) was already up, trimming his nails, and he went to look in
on [Seamus](../NPC%20Sheets/Seamus.md), whom he had put to bed in a cage with the moth. The cage was gone.
So was Seamus.

[Iter](../Player%20Sheets/Iter%20Bravus.md) had slipped away first, into mushrooms, and come out in a slate-grey
meadow with one scarlet figure in it: a short dress, a wide pointed hat, a
silver bauble. [The Red Lady](../NPC%20Sheets/Red%20Lady.md) was not doing well. [The Moon](../NPC%20Sheets/The%20Moon.md), she said, had
been wrapped up for a few years with one human man, and was waning more
than waxing. She did not know the sand. She did not know the figure with
the scales either, and when Iter described him she said, "Stay away from
him." She sighed at [Yuhai](../NPC%20Sheets/Yuhai.md)'s name, which Iter noted. She asked Iter to
keep an eye on it all, and to pry. Then, in the spirit of a patron who
cannot do much and wants to be seen doing something, she gave Iter a
glowing head, to be lit on command, and let her spore cloud learn to hurt
the people standing in it as well as heal the people next to them. Doing
both at once would take concentration. Doing it badly would heal everyone
or hurt everyone, and she was not promising which.

Iter and Pudge found the footprints first: large, bare, out of the door.
They were Bob's. The party took the news that he had walked out in the
night, undressed, with the calm of people who had known him a while. The
moth, asked, was less calm. [Lark](../Player%20Sheets/Lark%20Myca%20Roux.md) spoke to it, which took some doing, and
what it said came in signs: wind to the northeast, toward the moon; a hot
day in cold rain at Seamus's name; violence, and then flight; "enormous
munchkins" took him. Two directions, one toward Bob and one toward the Red
Line. It was not a clean answer. Lark said so.

[Alistrassa](../Player%20Sheets/Alistrassa.md) had spent the night better than anyone. The ROD, as she had
drawn it, was a segmented mirror-and-lens device that bends a column of
light, and a light source for it was a sum of thirty. She sent chits to
[Transmutation](../Lore/Places/Transmutation.md) for a heat-proof capsule that [Ra](../NPC%20Sheets/Ra.md) could be fired into, to
[Abjuration](../Lore/Places/Abjuration.md) and [Snarfa](../NPC%20Sheets/Snarfa.md) for wards and a light source and, if he knew one, a minor
celestial, and she asked [Penny](../NPC%20Sheets/Penny.md) to be her research associate. Three new books
were on their way. Snarfa's reply, when it came, was one line in the middle
of half a folio. She had not tranced in days. Her body made the point the
way bodies do, and she sat slightly to one side of her own thoughts.

Then she put the lens to [Ada](../NPC%20Sheets/Ada%20Crane.md)'s ledgers. The numbers and the words beside
them had been changed, mostly by transmutation, some by conjuration. A
mirror held up to the page showed the same lie, so it was not an illusion
in anyone's head. She poured her sand on a page. It swirled up and pulled
the ink back through grades of sand, line by line, until the page was
blank, and then, pressed into the paper where a pen had once leaned, the
true entries appeared. "Bardo material, one crate, two gold bars." Over
and over, down the ledger. A gold bar, the party was reminded, is the
scale of dowries, fiefs and an army's pay. Ada concluded that her books
were cooking themselves. Pudge concluded, of the sand and where it came
from, "Bob is a strategic resource." The sand that Bob had left beside
his tree, Ada priced at about seven gold pieces for under two grams.

[Martha](../NPC%20Sheets/Martha%20Woolsworth.md), at [Procurement](../Lore/Places/Procurement.md), answered Alistrassa's chit with an org chart and a transfer
form and a warning that it might be tricky. Alistrassa's chit to the
College library came back rude, signed by a librarian named [Jeremy](../NPC%20Sheets/Jeremy.md), and
a second, to the general index, came back with a book titled *Aspectual Harmonics*. The
party floated a handful of plans and dropped most of them. They would ask
Snarfa to come; the letter was written and not sent. They would glamour
someone as Finnegan to open the death rune; that one died on the observation
that it kills whoever they glamour. Pudge, pressed, recalled the
assasination rune at last, and rolled well enough to be sure of it: a professional's
rushed job on a quarter folio, not in the draftsman's own ink, quite
possibly with no countersign and no report back to the caster. The
underdean's chewing-out had been about the man and the place, not the
rune: a job he wanted off his plate. They carried the sand the way you
carry anything hot but quiet. The danger was a pickpocket.

Bob they found where the moth had said: asleep and naked under the squirrel
tree, with a little sand beside him. Iter swaddled him in her expanding
tent. Pudge took [The Bald One I Think](../NPC%20Sheets/Lark's%20Squirrels.md) from Lark, and carried Bob back to
[the College](../Lore/The%20College%20of%20Mages.md) rather than leave him there. Alistrassa scratched a ward in
the dirt on the way.

Back at the College, tensions flared. Iter wanted
Alistrassa to trance, and had gone to the clinic and come away with two
greater sleeping potions. Lark spotted it. There was a standoff about who
was standing where, in which Lark's read of the situation beat Iter's
bluff, though Lark believed, to the end, that it was tea. It was left
there. Pudge, in a reception, put Bob down on a sofa and found himself
looking at a wizard at a ledger, and an inkwell with black ink in it,
not blue. He was skittish, being back in the College, and he wanted, as
always, a full belly and for the responsibility to be somebody else's.
