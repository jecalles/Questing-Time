# Session Zero — the envelope run

*Played 2026-08-29. The campaign's first session. Next: Session One,
the Red Line and the Don.*

Four roads into the City. Iter Bravus led a blind man through the caves
under New Squishnesta for days, following a vision from the Red Lady —
find a man stumbling out of the depths — while the man, Baeon "Bob"
Oberoni, prayed to Yuhai and trusted a divining rock. Both came up into
daylight caked in cave and, in Bob's case, in the ash of his dead
priest, which he refuses to wash off. Lark Myca Roux floated out of the
Sharp Forest on the far edge of the Shire with five squirrels who had
fallen out of the trees under her aura, developed a drug problem, and
drunk her gas charges dry. Pudge sat in a College basement while a
nervous old wizard told him the College was "deeply interested in the
results" of a quest some strangers were about to take, and that his job
was to shadow them and see it succeed. The wizard was hiding something.
Pudge then sat in the Battered Drum for a day and a half, waiting.

They met at the Drum. So did Finnegan Bramblebush, a Munchkin already
high on Lark's gases from the road in — "Finnegan, you're one of us" —
who had a job: fetch a wax-sealed College envelope from the tavern in
Hamlet, a long day's walk northwest, and bring it back. The deed to his
new farmstead, he said. No fee was named. Nobody asked.

Nobody rested either. The party drank through the night, Bob and Iter
got drunk enough that every social roll went strange, and when Bob
prayed over the ash, Yuhai stood up out of it. The god was confused,
bluffed about the Red Lady from an old prayer book, recommended
short-term bonds, and gave Bob his charge — lead the Bobs in the light
of the moon — before settling back into a heap that Bob scooped into a
vial he now wears at his neck. Iter and Lark argued about the Red Lady
and whom she visits; Iter kept talking to learn more, and took the
disadvantage for it. Lark hotboxed the camp; Iter chose not to resist,
and tripped for most of the trip.

The walk was long. Hamlet came into view at the end of it, a handful of
buildings on the corner of a brook, a stone bridge into town, and
something wrong with it: a shiny mist over the whole village that Lark
felt as sharp and pointy, and lute music, somehow a bad cover of
itself, that only Iter could hear. The tavern was ordinary — a human
barkeep, farmers, a few Munchkins, and one man in black armour that ate
the light, one arm gone and the other rotting. Bob knew him. Shiny Bob,
from long ago, and dead, Bob had thought. Shiny Bob listened to
Finnegan brag and left early.

The envelope was handed over. Pudge broke the seal — no roll, just a
choice — and read it, with Bob burning four determination tokens to
help him understand what he was looking at. It was a death rune, one
line of common script and nothing else, written to kill whoever read
it. Anyone else would have died on the spot. Pudge's armour swallowed
the discharge and two of his spines cracked; everyone else felt it as a
sick lurch and took minor damage. The seal on the envelope matched the
seal over the College's own archway. Pudge, having read the thing meant
to kill his employer's courier, went cold on Finnegan for exactly as
long as it took to roll a natural 20 and charm him back. Finnegan is
smitten.

Then Bob mentioned that killing Finnegan "seems to be of interest to my
master," and Finnegan ran. Lark's vapour caught him at the door and
filled his head with the endless need to keep running. Bob smashed the
cage off his pack, and the moth inside reverberated the room until
Finnegan dropped convulsing; Bob got him by the beard and lost him.
Lark's hose missed; a squirrel bit his face. Iter put a mushroom the
size of a small tree up through the floor and Finnegan bounced off the
ceiling. Pudge grabbed, swung, and missed twice. And Bob — who had by
now decided he was protecting Finnegan — tripped over his own rope and
body-slammed him, which did the job: majorly hurt, bleeding, pinned,
and stuffed into Lark's moth cage with his limbs hanging out. Lark
dropped a smoke bomb. The party left the tavern wrecked.

Outside, Finnegan talked. The College promised him land for moving
their product — enchanted candies and syrupy confections — into the Red
Line. Bill the Butcher runs the eastern Red Line and the butterbeer and
butter-rum trade. Bramblebeard Thramplesnatch corners the butterscotch,
and is a pirate. Finnegan did not know what the envelope was. He does
now. The party walked out of Hamlet unpaid, carrying a live
assassination device, a Munchkin in a cage, a rusty hilt with no blade,
a half-filled spittoon, two more sealed scrolls nobody has opened, and a
long moonlit road back to the City, on which Iter was already getting
weaker.

## What your characters know

- The envelope is a College of Mages death rune: one-shot, kills the
 reader, only the reader. Its seal is the College's own. Pudge read it
 and lived because of his armour; nobody else could have.
- The College handed it to Finnegan as the deed to his farmstead. They
 meant him dead.
- Finnegan smuggles enchanted candy into the Red Line for the College,
 on the promise of land. He knows who moves what.
- Bill the Butcher: eastern Red Line, butterbeer and butter-rum.
 Bramblebeard Thramplesnatch: butterscotch, and a pirate.
- Shiny Bob — undead, black light-absorbing armour, one arm — heard
 everything Finnegan said in the tavern and left. Bob knew him alive.
- Pudge was sent by the College to see this quest succeed. The wizard
 who sent him was agitated and hiding something.
- The Red Lady's guidance to Iter is "it's about a place," not a man.
 Yuhai charged Bob to lead the Bobs in the light of the moon. The two
 seem to be pushing the same way.
- Hamlet sits under a shiny mist and, for Iter, bad lute music. Nobody
 knows why.
- Alignment runs between characters as well as with factions, and it is
 not mutual. Misaligned with someone means disadvantage to persuade or
 intimidate them.
- Nobody set a fee. Finnegan was the paymaster.
- Iter weakens as the moon wanes.
