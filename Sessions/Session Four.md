# Session Four — the couch, and Seamus

*Played 2026-09-24. Previously: Session Three, [the depot](../Lore/Places/The%20Red%20Line%20Depot.md) firefight and
the karmic list.*

It was the second half of October at [the College of Mages](../Lore/The%20College%20of%20Mages.md), and the end of
the semester was coming on. In [Abjuration](../Lore/Places/Abjuration.md), reputedly the calmest room in
the College and possibly the land, students switched from white tea to
black and walked, briskly, to fetch fresh ink. The party had the red
velvet couch in the middle of it, two bottles of [Snarfa](../NPC%20Sheets/Snarfa.md)'s sixty-year
Sylvan wine open, and [Finnegan Bramblebush](../NPC%20Sheets/Seamus.md) tied to one of its legs. [Pudge](../Player%20Sheets/Pudge.md)
had gone to answer the College's chit in person. [Munge](../NPC%20Sheets/Munge.md) was on his way out
to tell [the Don](../NPC%20Sheets/Don%20Aldo%20Zuccaro.md) everything.

[Iter](../Player%20Sheets/Iter%20Bravus.md) caught him at the door and re-explained themselves. The party
was grateful; the party would bring Finnegan along; the party simply
needed to heal first and get a little more out of him. She thanked Munge
with a hand on his arm, and he went off to the Don believing every word of
it and somewhat smitten.

Snarfa cauterised the new join on Iter's neck with a touch of his glowing
hand and remarked that she smelled of roast meats and sage. Then
Iter produced two blunderbusses from her skirt, taken from the train pirates,
and handed one to [Alistrassa](../Player%20Sheets/Alistrassa.md). Nobody had known they were up there. Alistrassa was
confident, for some time, that the bell end went against
the shoulder. Abjuration did not care for it either way: when Iter picked
it up the room's wards very nearly took it apart in her hands, and Bob,
who had met guns before in the Heart, lifted it by the barrel and nearly
dropped it. They carried it round the College looking for anyone who knew
how it worked. Nobody did. Wizards do not use guns. [Jeff](../NPC%20Sheets/Jeff.md), an [Evocation](../Lore/Places/Evocation.md)
student in athletic gear, had never heard the word blunderbuss and
suggested, helpfully, that they ask the Don. Snarfa jumped out of a window
to find out whether he could fly, and could not. Iter's spores patched him
up. He jumped out of it again and reported that he had got it this time.

Bob told the elves about dying — a moon goddess and a thing with more heads than it needed with the 
scales and one feather — and mentioned that he had snorted a good deal of the
sand before each death, along with some confectioner's sugar, which he
understood to be drugs for small people. The elves agreed that humans do
not, as a rule, die and come back, and that something about Bob was a
violation of the karmic order. His ears had come back mismatched, one very
small and one rather large, though it only really shows straight on.
Alistrassa opened the book she had found under the body of the last
person to hold her desk. Sixteen names were crossed out. A seventeenth was
circled. It said Bob. Iter looked for her own name and did not find it.

No two Bobs, it was established, share a face.

They woke Finnegan, who had spent the afternoon inching toward the door on
his leash, and Iter apologised and gave him wine. He had been thinking, he
said, and had arrived at the view that money makes people do things. The
candy trade, mostly. He knew the sand — he had taken it for candy; it
tasted terrible and got everywhere — and he knew it was bought legally, by
the College, through [Procurement](../Lore/Places/Procurement.md), and collected by a Munchkin with a long
moustache and a goatee whose name he had never learned. It came in with
Bramblebeard, who runs trains between Seaside and [New Squishnesta](../Lore/New%20Squishnesta.md) — Finnegan
pointed, and there was the sea, catching the late light, with a long train
running out beside it — and whose shipment the party had just blown up. As for who kills people in the
Red Line, that was the Don; it was how he kept the peace. Finnegan then
began on the dead, starting with Old Grampus, who used to sit by the well,
and went on, with dates, for something near a hundred names.

Alistrassa asked whether the College paid above market price, was told
that the market price of bat's wing is whatever the one man who has
it says it is, and concluded, aloud, that the College was laundering
money. She asked Snarfa for a privacy shield, got an iridescent blue dome,
and inside it said the thing she had been keeping back. It was Dean
[Alistair](../NPC%20Sheets/Dean%20Alistair.md), her own dean, who had sent her to find them. She was disposable.
The College was not safe, and at work she would have to pretend she had
never met any of them. 

The party thought about it very hard together and came away with a sense
of it: a lucrative arrangement, a loose end everybody knew about, and a
murder attempted with an assassination rune rather than two Munchkins in
an alley. Whoever had wanted Finnegan dead at [Hamlet](../Lore/Places/Hamlet.md), it was not the Don,
or the Don had not known.

Which still left Finnegan, whom everyone wanted dead. Snarfa had once
heard of a wizard bringing a man back from a fingernail. Alistrassa
proposed an arm. They would take one off, let the Don have Finnegan's
death, and have Snarfa raise him from the arm somewhere else, under a new
face, on an ordinary passenger train — not the pirates', who were
unlikely now to be friendly. Snarfa started costing blood diamonds and
goblin fangs and put it at about a week. Alistrassa offered to pay out of
her department. Finnegan was brought round to the idea that it might not
hurt. He was not persuaded that he would come back. By the end of the
session nobody had cut anything off.

Snarfa did the face first: bones shifted, nose flattened, the hair gone
blonde, every feature turned to its opposite. Finnegan found the process
more affecting than anyone would have liked. The new man needed a name, and got Seamus. Asked what he would
say if someone called him Seamus, he said, "My name's not Finnegan, it's
Seamus," which was nearly right, so Iter asked Snarfa to stop him saying
the word Finnegan. Snarfa waved his hands. Seamus could no longer say
anything at all. Iter felt this was fine. Alistrassa felt it was ideal.

The rest of the afternoon went to projects. [Penny](../NPC%20Sheets/Penny.md), a first-year in the
writing room who had been watching all day, agreed to put abjuration runes
on Iter's parasol in exchange for two books she needed for her exams, and
Iter fetched them. Alistrassa found she already knew the runes for
something much bigger — a shaft of lenses and a light, with mirrors to
throw a circle round her — and did not yet know how to build it, so she
settled for writing them on a cloak. She got the cloak, and some canvas,
from [Martha](../NPC%20Sheets/Martha%20Woolsworth.md) in Procurement on the College's account, and asked Martha to
look into the sand as the start of a paper. Martha said [Transmutation](../Lore/Places/Transmutation.md)
knew more about materials, and added, kindly, that Alistrassa would
probably die before she did. Snarfa, meanwhile, was on his phone. He was
throwing a party. He was very excited about someone called [Calliope](../NPC%20Sheets/Calliope.md). [Ra](../NPC%20Sheets/Ra.md)
was coming.
