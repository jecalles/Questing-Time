A faction is the primary unit of plot in this game. Locations list who operates there. Factions hold what those people want and what they do next.

Each entry uses this template:

- **Wants**: the standing goal. Present tense.
- **Fronts**: the locations the faction operates from.
- **Assets**: named characters, races, and resources.
- **Clues**: what a party can learn, and where.
- **Current Move**: the thing in motion this arc. This is the plot point slot.

A Current Move that outgrows its entry becomes its own page.

# New Squishnesta

## The College of Mages

Canonizes the Derived Magics. The dominant political faction of [New Squishnesta](../Lore/New%20Squishnesta.md).

The College is the seat of Derived magical power — transmutation and abjuration hold their strongest seat here — and the political faction that benefits most from the trains ([The Trains](../Lore/New%20Squishnesta.md#the-trains)). Founded by innate magic users, but now about Derived magic: out of tune with nature, in tune with industry and economy. Predominantly elves, with a number of humans; much of its rank and file is human. The upper ranks are elven as a rule — elves have simply been there longer — and a human tends to die before a deanship comes round. That is a political reality of the place. Its deterrent power is enough that crime in the City rarely touches a legal system at all — crossing even a minor adept is understood to be your own fault.

**Doctrine**: you can't cheat Derived magics — magic is derived from the true organization of matter and words, a truth in the fundamental magical language of the universe. A clear eye on how the world really is is therefore a high College priority. But the College is insular, and its biases keep it from seeing the truest form of that universal language — not for lack of trying. There are factions within the College (unspecified).

**Libraries**: semi-public libraries where common knowledge is held in common under College beneficence — a standard library, but written from the College's semi-colonial lens (readable as such with an Instinct or Wisdom roll against it) — plus secret internal libraries for the dark and restricted arts.

The College keeps many archaeologists and geologists, and is very interested in the cave system ([The UKaves](../Lore/Questing%20Time!%20Geography.md#the-ukaves)).

**Player-facing framing.** In play the College reads as "the Harvard of the wizarding world" — an educational body *and* a continent-spanning political power, governing the derived/book magics. Its seat sits beside the Castle in the capital; the seal above one of its archways is the same seal that was on the assassination envelope.

**The building.** A hexagon of six schools, a spire each, one quad, abutting the Castle — walked in full by [Alistrassa](../Player%20Sheets/Alistrassa.md) over four months. The place, its people and its chit economy are in [The College of Mages](../Lore/The%20College%20of%20Mages.md), which also holds its two governing bodies: the elven **Academic Council** of six deans, and the largely human **Bureaucratic Council** where the worldly power sits. One dean is named for the players: [Alistair](../Mechanics/Stat%20Blocks.md#dean-alistair) of Restoration. Snarfa, the super senior, has been a student for fifty years.

- **Wants**: TBD. Control of what counts as canon is the obvious answer.
- **Fronts**: a building abutting [the Castle](../Lore/New%20Squishnesta.md#key-locations) — not inside it.
- **Assets**: Derived casters ([Wizardry](../Mechanics/Abilities.md#wizardry)). Access to artifacts generated by the Illuminated ([Derived Magic](../Lore/Magic%20System.md#derived-magic)).
- **Clues**: TBD.
- **Current Move**: **the tidy after the Hamlet job.** The envelope was a one-shot death rune meant for Finnegan Bramblebush, and it failed (see below). Since then: the seal on the archway has been re-carved; the right meetings have been cancelled; a Castle investigator can be seen exploring the halls; and nobody inside will decide whether the loose ends are still worth killing. The party is the spanner in the works. Also live: the College is "doing some construction" and, by the Don's account, wants regulation pushed at city hall.

**What Session Zero exposed.** The party ran the envelope and opened it. It was a one-shot death rune — "a sloppy assassination," a single common-script death incantation — meant to kill whoever read it. It was handed to [Finnegan Bramblebush](../Mechanics/Stat%20Blocks.md#finnegan-bramblebush) disguised as "the deeds of [his] new farmstead": **the College tried to have Finnegan killed.** Finnegan is a magical smuggler who has been working with elements of the College — moving magical items, and mostly enchanted candy and syrupy confections (see [Enchanted confections — the butter trade](../Mechanics/Items%20and%20Equipment.md#enchanted-confections--the-butter-trade)), into [the Red Line](../Lore/New%20Squishnesta.md#key-locations) — for the promise of land. The party left Session Zero with hooks into this and into the College's Red Line money dealings; see [Where's the Money?](#wheres-the-money).

## The Five Families

Five crime families run the underground trade of [The Red Line](../Lore/New%20Squishnesta.md#key-locations), the City's Munchkin district. The archetype word is the family's label — the Butchers (colloquially; formally "Bill's family"), the Bootleggers, the Corner Crew, the Don's family (**La Familia Munquiana**, the Zuccaros), and the fifth family, the Millbanks — and bosses carry proper names that may or may not embed it. None of them controls it. None of them wants to — a fragmented Red Line means no single family is ever the only target. Every family specializes in one substance while still dabbling in the other two, and that overlap is most of what the small turf skirmishes are actually about. See [Substances](../Mechanics/Items%20and%20Equipment.md#substances).

**Munchkins born in the Red Line hold Aligned standing with all five families**, under the racial identity rule ([Racial Identity](../Mechanics/Questing%20Time!%20Mechanics.md#racial-identity)). Munchkins from Squishnestashire's farms hold Misaligned standing instead — the Red Line and the Homesteaders draw from the same people, and no longer agree on much.

### The Butcher

**Boss: Bill the Butcher** — named at Session Zero (GM: "we're just gonna fully steal the name Bill the Butcher"). **Sweet.** Beloved, a confectioner at heart stuck with a butcher's name, the closest thing the Red Line has to an honest family head. His family are **the makers**: the candy kitchens that refine boat ingredients into product, the dosing and batching rooms, the counting rooms, and the meat-cart logistics that move it — under a mostly legitimate food operation of real butchers, market stalls and provisioning, skewing older and domestic where the Corner Crew skews young. He is **the wholesale cut**: takes Bootlegger bulk, refines and packages it, sets the street price, supplies the corners. A literal butcher's shop in the **eastern** Red Line is the front, with a baker next door and a candlestick maker next to that; he also runs the butterbeer and butter-rum lines (see [Enchanted confections — the butter trade](../Mechanics/Items%20and%20Equipment.md#enchanted-confections--the-butter-trade)).

His sweetness is his armour: free meat on feast days, debts he forgets, a district that would riot before giving him up. His real leverage over the other families is not the recipe (wizards can reverse-engineer a recipe) but **trust**: Bramblebeard trusts nobody else, the corners run his product because it is the best, and the Don and Mrs Millbank prize his stability. It would be a shame if Bill ended up dead.

Martha Woolsworth, the College's procurement clerk, is his sister; he knows where she clerks and not what the money is. Finnegan expects Bill to kill him on sight — and sweet Bill would, personally and hot. Bill owes Finnegan a week's wages. The Don sends people to Bill for provisions; the party has that introduction.

- **Wants**: hold the neighbourhood's love, and the cut.
- **Fronts**: the shop and the kitchens, eastern Red Line.
- **Assets**: [Bill the Butcher](../Mechanics/Stat%20Blocks.md#bill-the-butcher). The makers. The distribution network. Trust.
- **Clues**: the counting rooms hold the wholesale numbers; the meat carts move product.
- **Current Move**: provisioning, as always.

### The Don

**La Familia Munquiana** — the **Zuccaro** family. **Boss: Don Aldo Zuccaro** — [Don Aldo Zuccaro](../Mechanics/Stat%20Blocks.md#don-aldo-zuccaro). The **old blood**: hereditary land, and deeds that quietly trace back to before the Castle, from the generations when Munchkins kept their own records. This is the family that keeps the records. Formal codes and a veneer of respectability laid over a business line of real brutality. Most likely of the five to sit down and negotiate, and most likely to mean it only until it stops being convenient. One wide family with several sugar-named cousin lines — del Sucre among them; Doucet, Praslin, Zuckermann, Sakharov are available and not yet used.

The Don owns everything that does not move:

- **The peace.** His family ships nothing, makes nothing, moves nothing. It holds the codes: turf lines drawn in his parlour, disputes settled at his table, a tithe from the three working families for the service — the tax you pay so the Red Line does not eat itself.
- **The land.** The kitchens cook, the corners stand, the warehouses store on his deeds.
-
-

**Met at Session One (2026-09-02).** The house: old stone in the district's oldest street, red leather, a sugar-plum cigar, a cat, cognac older than the party, a library of old leather. The crew: **Ziggy** (the talker, sent on errands), **Slo Mo** (never hurries), **Tommy Two Cheeks** (quiet, a dent in his head; now with the party). **Munge**, an eight-foot half-giant, is the family's muscle and was beaten nearly to death in the raid. **Nonna**, the Don's great aunt, runs the kitchen. The Don paid the party 100 gold and called them friends of the family: **Ally** toward Iter, Bob and Lark, **Aligned** toward Pudge. He does not do violence by hand, or in the house. "We kill people for the Red Line. We get paid to do it."

- **Wants**: the peace, held; the land, unexamined; the College kept to the arrangement.
- **Fronts**: the parlour; a safe house a few doors down that the party now uses.
- **Assets**: [Don Aldo Zuccaro](../Mechanics/Stat%20Blocks.md#don-aldo-zuccaro), [Ziggy](../Mechanics/Stat%20Blocks.md#ziggy), [Slo Mo](../Mechanics/Stat%20Blocks.md#slo-mo), [Tommy Two Cheeks](../Mechanics/Stat%20Blocks.md#tommy-two-cheeks), [Munge](../Mechanics/Stat%20Blocks.md#munge); four more armed Munchkins in the front room; the archive. Retainer pay for people who do steady work.
- **Clues**: the archive ();.
- **Current Move**: get Finnegan back. The Don blames the pirates and has lent the party Tommy. He has hinted at work at city hall for the College's "construction."

### The Corner Crew

Young, loose, loyal to a block rather than a bloodline. **The retail layer** — corner kids, boots on the ground, moving candy on the street (GM terminology: "corner kids"). No hierarchy but **the room**, which is a mechanic, not a metaphor: crews compare take and respect at a monthly count, and whoever earned it holds the room until the next one. A chair, not a crown — arrest the boss and next month there is another. Each corner is its own crew of lookouts, runners and count-kids; block loyalty outranks family loyalty. Names run to one-worders and ironic handles. Less a political faction than "the disenfranchised youth of the Red Line organised into a gang structure," anarchic and emergent; the model is The Wire, docks to corners to the money to the government. They sell taffy, chocolate, butterscotch and butter rum. They do not sell the Sour Patch mushroom, though they have heard of it, and what reaches the street with it in it is a pressed pill cut with other things ([Sour Patch Mushroom](../Mechanics/Items%20and%20Equipment.md#sour-patch-mushroom)).

**Ruby** holds the room, three counts running — unprecedented, and the older families have noticed. She keeps her own book, and it has taught her that the street's take does not match what flows back down: a corner kid asking "where's the money" from the bottom while the party asks it from the top. Future informant or future warlord, depending on who reaches her first. Her second is **Deuce**. **Lil Mikey**, a half-giant with a kinship to the corner kids, runs with them.

The corners are where alignment runs thinnest: the arrests, the violence and the dependency land on their blocks. The Aligned/Misaligned seam between Red Line and Homestead Munchkins is most crossable here. Corner kids will work with anyone who has good product; Bill's is the best.

- **Wants**: survive, and stay loyal to each other while doing it.
- **Fronts**: the corners, re-upping at dusk.
- **Assets**: the default [Munchkin block](../Mechanics/Stat%20Blocks.md#the-munchkin). Numbers, and street-level trust. Ruby's book.
- **Clues**: Ruby's book; the count.
- **Current Move**: holding the room, and starting to wonder where the money goes. Lark means to meet Ruby (Letters from Lark, letter one).

### The Bootleggers

The Bootleggers are **train pirates**. the Bootleggers move goods inland by rail and cut the cars off at the **train depots** — one sits at an edge of [the Red Line](../Lore/New%20Squishnesta.md#key-locations), opposite the Castle, rusted and half-disused. **Bulk import**: the family's business is volume, the waterline and the rail, plus warehousing; it ends where Bill's begins. They bring in whatever a run can source — an unstable, opportunistic supply of raw ingredients — Pirates to a one, willing to lose an eye over a few gold coins. Names are Munchkin mouthfuls with a pirate's swagger.

**Boss: Bramblebeard Thramplesnatch** — not "Bramblebush," as Session Zero had it. A jovial, heavy-drinking Munchkin who **corners the butterscotch trade** (see [Enchanted confections — the butter trade](../Mechanics/Items%20and%20Equipment.md#enchanted-confections--the-butter-trade)); dangerous to drink against — "go toe to toe with him you might lose a tooth." He trusts nobody outside his own, except Bill. **He is [Finnegan Bramblebush](../Mechanics/Stat%20Blocks.md#finnegan-bramblebush)'s half-brother** — one Munchkin parent of the Bramble line, different other parents, different surnames — which is why the street mixes the two names up and why the Bootleggers tolerate Finnegan at all. "You can find Bramblebeard by the docks" — the depot's loading dock, in Red Line speech.
- **Assets**: [Bramblebeard Thramplesnatch](../Mechanics/Stat%20Blocks.md#bramblebeard-thramplesnatch). The rail line.
- **Clues**: the manifests — how cargo is labelled, how cars go missing.

### The Millbanks

The fifth family: the **old money**, and the one that faces outward. Their deeds are fresh from the Castle, notarised and up to date; theirs is the connection to the new government, which nobody but the old families thinks is too new. They are the Red Line's only direct line to the New Squishnesta government (which is not the College), and their irreplaceable thing is **legitimacy** in the larger political order — which is why the books run through their outfit. Register: Gilded Age Anglosphere aristocracy.

**Temperament**: pragmatists in a colonial setting — self-interested, predictable, reasonable, ruthless, and therefore betrayal-proof: they know the setup is stable, so any deal that disrupts it, even to their benefit, is presumed false. Typical Munchkins are ebullient; this family has a reserved pleasantness, quiet and cheery, that other Munchkins register as different. The reserve is the tell.

**The Ceding.** At some point the family gave up a third of the Red Line, including some of their own people, to keep the peace — they **brokered the construction of the Red Line itself**. The district was once open; enclosure was the alternative to exile — or worse. The third they gave up is the third that fell outside the new line; the district's name is the wall's name. Millbank is one of the ceded streets — the family name survives only on the third they gave away.

**Mrs Winifred Millbank** is the matriarch — "Miss Millbank" since childhood, "Mrs Millbank" today; nobody would deign to call her Auntie. Duty as identity, the institution wearing a person. Her heir is her granddaughter **Winnie**: fierce, intense, clear-eyed, righteous, daring — the best chance the Red Line has, and a potential ally for the party. Two Winnies: the wall, and a door that is ajar and burning.

- **Wants**: the arrangement held; the remittance paid; the family name kept clean on paper.
- **Fronts**: the parlour. The Castle, through the licence.
- **Assets**: the default [Munchkin block](../Mechanics/Stat%20Blocks.md#the-munchkin). The tax farm. The Financier, working out of the outfit. Standing with the government.
- **Clues**: the remittance record — public-ish Castle paper, the one thread from the Red Line into the government that never passes through the College.
- **Current Move**: TBD.

## The Unseelie Fey

The Court of the Unseelie have long known the magics of this land — if not their origin, then at least their effect. The Seelie know the land exists. They do not know it matters. The Unseelie mean to keep it that way.

They are Dark Fae of the Unseelie Court, whose seat is **Pandemonium** in the [Feywild](../Lore/Magic%20System.md#the-feywild) — and, like every Fae but one, they live half there and half here. What they have in the Sharp Forest is a push, not a home. The Forest's own Fae are [Razor's Edge](#razors-edge), who keep no court, are no kin of theirs, and are the only Fae people who live wholly on Gaia.

- **Wants**: keep the Forest's importance secret from the Seelie.
- **Fronts**: Pandemonium, their seat in the [Feywild](../Lore/Magic%20System.md#the-feywild); the Sharp Forest, where the expansion is pushing in. The Well ([New Squishnesta](../Lore/New%20Squishnesta.md)) is confirmed as a contact point — but for **all** Fae, Seelie included, not an Unseelie-only channel. It leads toward both the Seelie Court and the Unseelie Court, and more broadly into the Feywild.
- **Assets**: stat blocks TBD. Distinct from [Fey (Seelie)](../Mechanics/Stat%20Blocks.md#fey-seelie).
- **Clues**: TBD.

## Razor's Edge

Robyn's people. They are **Forest Fae** — the Fae of the Sharp Forest, who keep no court — and Razor's Edge is their faction, named live at the table by the player behind [Robyn](../Player%20Sheets/Lark%20Myca%20Roux.md). Alone among the Fae they live wholly on Gaia, with no second residence in the [Feywild](../Lore/Magic%20System.md#the-feywild); they return there only when they die. Robyn named them for that edge: they are constantly on the razor's edge of magic, and well aware of the thin line between life and death. They move through the Forest on a calendar that shifts with the seasons. Over generations, as separate communities merged, their migration loop has pulled inward — a smaller and smaller pattern, further each generation from the outer Forest.

They take the name from **the Razor's Edge itself** — a large cliff face at the far inner edge of that old range, toward the silicon interior, where the land drops away in thin waterfalls, roughly 1,000 feet down into a fine mist. It was a seasonal gathering ground, its rim wide enough to hold whole congresses. The loop has since pulled inward and almost no one travels that far out now. Robyn camped there alone as a child and saw things she did not yet understand.

Explicitly unaligned with either the Seelie Court or the Unseelie Court — membership runs by people and region, not by court politics — and holds its own internal politics and infighting, unspecified. They are not a splinter of either, and no kin of [the Unseelie](#the-unseelie-fey), who are Dark Fae from another domain, pushing in. Robyn's parents work as traveling ambassador-diplomats between the two Courts and the region's cities; the GM ties Robyn's own nomadic, knowledge-sharing streak directly to them. The people are also known for a "smart material" — silica and rare metals worked with space-bending transmutation magic into a fabric that is very strong, very thin, very warm, and velvet-soft — made through a craft tradition split area by area across the bands.

**A Razor's Edge Fae can be patroned by the Sharp Forest without being a Druid of the Forest.** [Lark](../Player%20Sheets/Lark%20Myca%20Roux.md) is: the Forest chooses her tattoos and delegates her gas-and-powder magic, the same channel it uses for [the Druids](#the-druids-of-the-forest). See [Aspects and the Non-Illuminated](../Lore/Magic%20System.md#aspects-and-the-non-illuminated).

- **Wants**: TBD.
- **Fronts**: the nomadic bands moving through the Forest's middle ring; the Razor's Edge cliff itself, now rarely visited.
- **Assets**: Robyn's family; the "smart material" craft tradition; a Forest-delegated Gifted line (via Lark). The Forest Fae block is unwritten, and it is theirs, not the Unseelie's.
- **Clues**: TBD.
- **Current Move**: TBD.

## The Anachrons

Silicon automata imbued with the vibrance of the Forest, but with no connection to Nature. Lawful Neutral, and mechanically unempathetic to the concerns of the Living. They are said to hold the secrets of Chronomancy, a mythical subfield of Conjuration that bends the flow of time.

**On wanting anything at all.** The Anachrons are effectively non-sentient — automata following their original programming rather than agents with goals. What they "care about" is really just what they were built to protect, which traces back to whoever built them (unwritten). Most that a party meets are unfeeling guardians defending a place. "What do they want" isn't quite the right question to ask of them.

- **Wants**: nothing, properly speaking — see above. What they do traces to their original programming, not a goal.
- **Fronts**: the deep Forest. The monoliths.
- **Assets**: Chronomancy, unconfirmed. Stat blocks TBD.
- **Clues**: TBD.
- **Current Move**: TBD.
